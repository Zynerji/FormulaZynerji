> **Reference digest — not part of the ruleset.** How the real FIA F1 *Financial* Regulations (the cost cap) and the ATR work, gathered to inform `regulations/financial.md`. Based on Section D 2026 (Issue 4) and reporting. Compiled May 2026 with citations.

# F1 Financial Regulations — Reference Digest

## 1. Document structure
Section D (F1 Teams, ~150 pp) + Section E (PU Manufacturers). Key articles: Definitions (Relevant Costs, Reporting Group, FYRD); Scope/Reporting Group; The Cost Cap; Relevant Costs (positive list); Exclusions (negative list); Adjustments (inflation, FX, high-cost jurisdiction, amortization); Capital Expenditure; Reporting (FYRD deadlines, signatures); Administration; Breach definitions; Accepted Breach Agreement; Adjudication Panel; Sanctions. Heavy use of capitalised Defined Terms — every inclusion/exclusion hinges on a definition.

## 2. The cap
| Season | Base cap | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $145M | launch |
| 2022 | $140M | |
| 2023 | $135M | target |
| 2024 | $135M + ~$1.8M/race >21 | ~$140.4M effective |
| 2025 | $135M + inflation | ~$140–142M |
| 2026 | **$215M** | mostly accounting reclassification, not real increase |

2026 changes: separate $36M 4-yr CapEx cap **abolished** (depreciation now inside the cap); staff must be 100%-allocated to F1 to count; new exclusions (health & safety, catering); high-cost-jurisdiction uplift (Audi/Sauber, Switzerland).

**Included:** all staff except top-3 + drivers; car parts; aero; wind tunnel/CFD; simulator; track ops; race travel; R&D/manufacturing; depreciation (2026).
**Excluded:** driver salaries; 3 highest-paid staff; marketing/PR; hospitality; legal/HR/finance/governance; heritage; non-F1 activities; PU purchase (separate cap); entry fees; health & safety (2026); catering (2026).
Carry-forward of up to $2M unused.

## 3. ATR — the parity sliding scale
Six ATR periods/yr. Baseline per period: **320 WT runs, 80 wind-on hrs, 400 hrs occupancy, 2,000 CFD items.** Multiplier by Constructors' position (5% steps): 1st 70% → 7th 100% → 10th/new-entrant 115%.

2025 H2 example: McLaren (1st) 224 WT runs / 1,400 CFD; Racing Bulls (7th) 320 / 2,000; Alpine (10th) 368 / 2,400. New entrant Cadillac = 115%.

Reset twice/yr (season start on prior standings; ~Jun 30 on current). ATR breach = **sporting** violation (Section F), penalised via stewards. ATR reduction is itself used as a penalty (Red Bull −10% for 12 months).

## 4. Compliance & penalties
FYRD due **31 March** post-season, with signed audit certificate. Review ~7 months (results Sep–Oct). On-site audits twice yearly for complex structures.

**Breach tiers:** Procedural (reporting failure); Minor overspend (<5%); Material overspend (≥5%). Procedural/minor may settle via **Accepted Breach Agreement** (no appeal; can't include points deduction/future-cap cut). Material → **Adjudication Panel** (6–12 members) with full menu: reprimand, fine, future-cap reduction, ATR reduction, activity suspension, points deduction, season exclusion.

**Real cases:** Red Bull 2021 — procedural + minor overspend (£1.864M, 1.6%); penalty $7M fine + 10% ATR reduction for 12 months (no bad faith found). Aston Martin 2024 — procedural (audit certificate 2 weeks late). Williams/Alpine — procedural, small fines via ABA. No confirmed material (>5%) breach to date.

## 5. Design lessons for an open formula
1. Single-tier cap with broad positive definition + explicit exclusions (robust to unanticipated tech).
2. **ATR sliding scale as primary parity lever** — set the range (70–115% or tighter), the currency (WT/CFD/test/dyno), reset frequency, new-entrant bonus.
3. Separate annual cap from multi-year CapEx (avoid depreciation complexity; CapEx cap is a finer scalpel against factory arms races).
4. Exclude driver + a few top staff salaries (prevents salary-dumping); cap development, not people.
5. Make penalties proportionate to advantage gained; **ATR reduction hurts a winner more than a fine.**
6. Reduce audit lag (12-month lag = a full season of advantage); add quarterly estimates / ATR escrow.
7. **Address related-party transactions from day one** (biggest F1 loophole) — markup standard or independent valuation.
8. Consider a minimum-spend floor (cap lifts the ceiling but not the floor).
9. Separate chassis vs powertrain caps (Sections D/E) if propulsion is a key innovation axis.
10. Publish a multi-year cap trajectory at launch (F1's improvised indexation + the 2026 jump hurt credibility).
